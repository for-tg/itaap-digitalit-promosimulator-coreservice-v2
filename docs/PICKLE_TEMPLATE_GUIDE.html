01 The shape

The pickle is the market. One dict carrying a weekly SKU panel, a fitted elasticity model, the name lists that model was fitted with, and a cost table. The backend loads exactly one at startup model_loader.py:358-414 and holds it in module globals for the life of the process. No database sits behind the simulation maths.

Everything that differs between markets therefore has to travel inside the artifact. That is the whole contract: a small fixed skeleton the backend can rely on, and a declared payload the market owns.

LayerDecided byContents
fixedthis contract Six top-level keys, fourteen structural panel columns, two cost columns. The same names in every market.
dynamicthe market Every model feature, every confounder, and the labels, rates and week windows that genuinely differ — declared in feature_names, confounder_names and meta.

Anything that is the same in every market stays in the code. meta carries only what a market actually changes — see the exclusions at the end of §4.

03 Dynamic

Features and confounders are not prescribed. What is mandatory is that the artifact declares them; what goes in the declaration is the market's own modelling decision. Two markets can share nothing but the skeleton and both be valid.

Declared inContainsContract requires
feature_names Every X column in fit order — one-hots, event flags, promo-history features, anything else the market modelled unique strings; length equals the model's X width; equals model._input_names
confounder_names Every W column — media, seasonality, competitor pressure, or none each a numeric, non-null panel column; length equals the model's W width
feature_spec.categorical Columns that were one-hot encoded: Classification always, plus a family or platform column if the market has one constant per SKU, non-null, strictly coarser than SKU
feature_spec.numeric Columns passed into X as they are numeric and non-null on every row, history and plan
event_flags The market's retail calendar, one flag_* column per event 0/1 floats on every row; at least one, or the engine stamps the Czech calendar on promo_engine.py:176-183

Two constraints on what you declare

  • A categorical must group SKUs, never be one. One-hot the SKU itself and the forest splits on product identity instead of pricing behaviour. The reference build hit this and had to drop two families, one holding ~0.82 of feature importance alone notebook cell 72. For scale, 6 438 history rows over 78 SKUs is a median of 44 rows per product against 104 per family. Rule P7 rejects a categorical with as many levels as there are SKUs.
  • The panel may not produce a one-hot column the model has never seen — the engine raises on it promo_engine.py:208-210. The reverse is safe: names in feature_names that the panel lacks are filled with 0.0. So feature_names, not the panel, is the authority on X.

The one-hot reference level is not declared anywhere: drop_first=True always removes the lexicographically first label, so it is sorted(segment_labels)[0] — "HRTB" for CZ. The validator derives and reports it rather than asking the artifact to repeat it.

05 Maintenance

Not part of the file format and not a backend document — this is the working rule for whoever owns a market artifact once the refactor has landed. Every change in the table below is made by rebuilding the pickle, not by editing app/.

One sentence keeps it from rotting: the pickle declares, the code reads. No market fact belongs in app/, and no modelling choice belongs anywhere but feature_names, confounder_names and meta.

ChangeWhat you rebuildBackend code
Add or drop a model featurepanel column → feature_spec → refit → revalidateuntouched
Add or drop a confounderpanel column → confounder_names → refituntouched
Change an event calendarmeta.event_flags → rebuild the flag columnsuntouched
Correct a VAT or trade-spend ratemeta.economics → rebuilduntouched
Rename a price bandmeta.segment_labels → refit, since the one-hot names changeuntouched
Plan a 53-week yearmeta.weeks_per_year → rebuild the spine and the plan yearuntouched
Onboard a market§6, then §7untouched
Change the contract itselfbump schema_version, update the validator and this page togetherloader gate
  • The validator is the gate. Run it as the last cell of the build and again in the deployment pipeline. A pickle that has not passed does not ship.
  • Version the contract, not the market. schema_version moves when the required shape changes, so the loader can refuse an artifact it is too old to read. A market rebuilding to the same shape does not bump it.
  • Rebuild is the update path. Because economics live in the artifact, changing a rate means rebuilding it — the deliberate cost of a self-describing pickle. The alternative was a second config file that drifts out of sync.
  • Keep the build parameters with the build. They are out of the contract, so the notebook is now the only record of the base-price window, the noise threshold and the seed. Losing it means the artifact cannot be reproduced.
  • One market per process, today. init_engine writes module globals behind a one-shot guard promo_engine.py:130-142, so a second call is a silent no-op. Serving two markets from one process needs that state moved into an instance.

06 Build steps

Execution order, market-agnostic. Five phases, seventeen steps. The parameters named along the way are not carried in meta, so record them in the build itself — a constant block at the top of the notebook — or the artifact cannot be reproduced.

A · Panel
01Load the sell-out panel

Units and price per SKU per week from whatever source the market uses, plus each SKU's price band.

checkRow and SKU counts tie to the source extract.
02Resolve one quantity series

Pick the authoritative volume column, forward-fill within each SKU, fall back to the secondary series where it is still missing, and null out any SKU on a known-wrong unit scale rather than letting it distort elasticities.

checkNo nulls remain and total volume ties to the source.
03Build the week spine and classify gaps

Cross-join every SKU with every year and every week from 1 to that year's week count — 53 in an ISO year that has one. Label each missing row pre-launch, post-exit or mid-life gap; fill the mid-life gaps only and mark them; drop the other two.

checkGrain is unique on (SKU, Year, Week) and no year is silently truncated at 52.
04Fix the segment, and any grouping attribute

Attach Classification. Add a family or platform column only if the market has one whose levels hold several SKUs each. Both must be constant for a SKU across its whole life.

checkEvery SKU maps to one segment and one level of each categorical.
B · Signals
05Derive the observed discount

Per SKU take the rolling maximum price over the previous N weeks, shifted by one so the current week cannot set its own reference, then raw_discount = clip(1 − Price / rolling_max, 0). This is what defines "on promotion" for the rest of the build.

checkValues in [0, 1), and the window carries across a year boundary.
06Define the event calendar

One flag_* column per retail event. Compute moving events per year from a calendar, never from a fixed week number. This is where a market is genuinely itself — do not port another market's calendar.

checkEach flag is on in exactly its declared weeks, in every year.
07Attach confounders

Media pressure, seasonality, competitor price and promo intensity — whatever the market can source, or nothing at all. Map external calendars onto (Year, Week) with an explicit reference table rather than inferring week numbers from dates.

checkZero nulls after the merge and the row count is unchanged.
C · Model
08Compute treatment and outcome

log_price and log_qty from clipped positives, so the estimated effect reads directly as an elasticity.

checkBoth finite wherever non-null.
09Build the model features

Whatever this market models — typically promo history (on promo last week, length of the current run, weeks since the last one), a historical promo-intensity profile by segment and week, and a seasonal volume index normalised within segment.

checkNo nulls, float dtype throughout — an object-dtype boolean survives .astype(float) as NaN.
10Assemble X and W

get_dummies on the categoricals with drop_first=True, concatenated with the numeric features. Keep X as a named DataFrame.

checkZero NaNs in X and W; both lists recorded into feature_spec.
11Fit the causal model

Optionally exclude families that dominate feature importance, then tune and fit a CausalForestDML with boosted nuisance models. Fit from the named frame so the model stores its own column names.

checkNuisance R² for Y and T inspected; a weak T model is an endogeneity warning, not a success.
D · Base values
12Derive the base price

Cap the discount, then Price_base = Price / (1 − capped) on promo weeks and Price elsewhere; finally take the element-wise maximum with Price.

check(Price_base < Price).sum() == 0.
13Synthesise the base quantity

Run the model backwards over its own panel: Qty_base = Qty / exp(effect(X, log Price_base, log Price)). Clamp down to observed only on non-promo or imputed rows; flag the rest for review rather than hiding them.

checkQty_base > 0 everywhere; remaining violations counted and reviewed.
14Assemble panel_df

Select the structural columns under the contract names, plus the declared flags, features and confounders. Round prices to price_decimals and never round quantities.

checkLinear and log columns still agree after rounding — see §11.
E · Package
15Extend to the plan year

Cross-join each SKU with every week of the plan year, carry confounders and base values forward from the last complete history year matched on (SKU, Week), reset the lag features to their cold-start values, and re-derive the event flags from the calendar instead of copying them.

checkPlan rows = SKUs × weeks_per_year; no nulls in any feature; flags correct per week.
16Build the cost table

Reindex any FX series to daily and forward-fill before joining, filter the price reference to one snapshot date, convert to the panel currency, merge COGS, collapse to one row per SKU.

checkZero null COGS, all positive, one row per panel SKU.
17Compose meta and save

Fill the template from §4 — all twenty-eight fields — then pickle the six keys.

checkpython docs/validate_pickle.py <path.pkl> exits 0.

07 Checklist

  • One row per SKU × Year × Week, no duplicates, Week an integer.
  • All fourteen structural columns present under the contract names.
  • Price, Qty and COGS on one price level, one currency.
  • Price_base ≥ Price, Qty_base > 0, quantities not rounded.
  • Log columns still equal the logs of their linear counterparts after rounding.
  • Every declared feature, flag and confounder column exists, is numeric, and is non-null on every row of history and plan.
  • Categoricals group SKUs; none has as many levels as there are SKUs.
  • Model fitted from a named DataFrame; X and W widths match the two name lists.
  • One positive cost row per panel SKU.
  • Plan-year rows complete for every SKU across weeks_per_year, actuals null, flags re-derived from the calendar.
  • meta complete — all twenty-eight fields of §4.
  • Validator exits 0.

09 Static today

What is written into the backend as a literal and has to come from the artifact instead. Pointers only — the refactor itself is separate work.

  • The confounder list. The engine refuses to start unless the pickle's confounder_names matches its own literal list. This is the first thing a second market hits.
  • The feature list. Sixteen feature names and the categorical pair are written into the feature-matrix code, so any market with different flags fails on a missing column.
  • The Czech event calendar. Stamped onto any panel that arrives without those eight flag columns, plus a fixed Easter lookup table.
  • The three price-band labels. Repeated across nine locations in filters, validation messages and request defaults.
  • The economic rates. VAT, trade spend, TPW, A&P, OFSE and the legacy trade cost.
  • The year literals. History and plan years as constants and Pydantic defaults, plus the 52-week assumption in every week loop and accumulator.
  • The optimizer bounds. Discount grid, uplift clip, peak-week multipliers, mandatory campaign weeks.
  • The currency. Symbol and decimal formatting in the generated insight copy, and the pickle filename standing in for the market's identity.

Quarter boundaries, the "Full year" label and the confidence-tier labels stay hardcoded on purpose — they do not vary by market. See the exclusions in §4.

11 Defects found

Found while validating. Four are inside the CZ pickle, written there by the build; the fifth is in the backend. Reported, not fixed — no artifact and nothing under app/ was modified.

1 · Qty_base is zero on 38 rowsin the pickle

The build rounds base quantity to whole units, so anything under half a unit becomes 0. The engine takes log() of it unguarded promo_engine.py:394, giving -inf in every uplift and profit figure for those SKU-weeks.

2 · The linear and log base quantities disagree on 35% of rowsin the pickle

That rounding is applied to Qty_base but not to log_qty_base, so the two columns describe different quantities on 3 682 of 10 494 rows, up to a factor of 1.96 apart. The engine reads whichever exists promo_engine.py:845, 860-866, so two code paths return different base volumes for the same row. The affected rows have a median base of 7 units, where rounding is a large relative error.

3 · Plan-year event flags are copied, not re-derivedin the pickle

Plan rows are built from the previous year's template and patched with ffill().bfill() per SKU. 44 of 78 SKUs lack a full history year, so their missing weeks inherit a neighbour's flag values: 122 plan weeks are flagged for events that are not happening and 261 real event weeks are left unflagged. Those flags are model inputs.

4 · The 2026 plan year is missing its last weekin the pickle

The build lays a fixed 52-week spine. ISO 2026 has 53 weeks, because 1 January 2026 is a Thursday — the panel stops at week 52 and the final trading week of the plan year has nowhere to go. The same applies to 2020 and will recur; it is a property of the year, not of the market, which is why weeks_per_year stays in meta.

5 · The calendar exists in two places and has driftedin the backend

The build and the engine each define the Czech events. Christmas is weeks 49–52 in the build but 49–51 in the engine promo_engine.py:160; the engine's Easter table gives 2025 as weeks 15–16 when Easter Monday falls in ISO week 17 and the panel itself carries 16–17. Once meta.event_flags is the only definition, the engine-side copy can be deleted.

12 Open questions

  • Price level. Are Price and COGS sell-out including VAT, or something else? The Triple Net bridge assumes they are comparable promo_engine.py:257-269 and no data can prove it. Not carried in meta because it is a global convention rather than a market choice — but it is still unwritten anywhere.
  • Legacy rates. Are TPW 1%, A&P 16%, OFSE 7% and the 24% trade cost still current, and are they per-market or global? Treated as per-market here.
  • Peak weeks. Should peak_week_priority and mandatory_campaign_weeks be a commercial input or derived from data? The CZ values are unattributed constants.
  • Partial plan years. The engine assumes the plan year is complete. Nothing in the artifact says where real coverage stops, and a coverage block was left out for want of a consumer. It is needed the day a market ships a partially observed plan year.
  • An unused guardrail. The CZ cost table carries a MaxPromoDiscount column — 13 values from 0 to 0.333, stored as strings — that nothing reads. It looks like a cap on discount depth, but there is no code path to confirm it.
